01 · Product promise

Personal Brain should learn your working preferences through a controlled feedback loop: collect meaningful evidence, consolidate it, condense it into useful knowledge, apply it in the right context, evaluate the result, and forget what is unwanted or obsolete.

This specification is for the product owner, designers and implementers. It proposes an evolution of “your working preferences, applied consistently across AI tools.” Persistent memory supports that promise; it does not imply model training or autonomous modification of the product’s own source code.

Proposed decision D-01: Learn automatically into candidates. Activate inferred behavioral changes after review. An explicit “remember this” may activate directly when the user’s scope and meaning are clear. Preserve an undo path.

Status: Proposed product requirements, 10 November 2026. Not an implementation audit or a claim that these features already exist. Examples below are illustrative interpretations of this conversation, not new saved preferences.

Applying formatter rules, changing repository files or installing skills remains a separate authorized implementation action.

F02 · Remember / forget commandsSay “remember this for Python” or “forget this preference” and see the interpreted scope.Clear explicit requests act within scope; ambiguous targets are clarified; outcome and undo availability are shown.

F07 · ConsolidationRelated observations become one clear proposal.Merge preserves all source references, exceptions and dissent; user can inspect or split the proposal.
F08 · CondensationKeep my active instructions short without losing meaning.Before/after view names preserved obligations and exceptions; failing semantic regression examples blocks activation.
F09 · Conflict resolutionSee competing rules and choose the intended context.System proposes keep, replace or scope-split; unresolved equally applicable contradictions remain visible.
F10 · Learning inboxReview useful batches without approval fatigue.Filter by project, class, status and evidence; each bulk action has a preview and per-item result.

F12 · Contextual applicationUse the right preferences in each agent and task.Only authorized scope and applicable approved versions are included; package records omitted rules and reasons.
F13 · ExplainabilityAsk “why did you do this?” or “why was this rule missing?”Trace shows source version, selection reason, overrides, exclusions and integration capability.
F14 · EvaluationLearn whether a change improved outcomes.Outcome links to the actual applied package and task; tests alone cannot prove personal preference alignment.
F15 · ForgettingSuppress unwanted suggestions, archive obsolete rules, or delete retained content.Impact preview names derivatives; active retrieval excludes target immediately after committed suppression.

05 · Evidence, confidence and scope

SignalTreatment
Explicit instructionHighest authority for the stated scope. Scope expansion requires another decision.
Repeated correctionSuggest a durable preference when evidence comes from independent tasks. Repeated copies do not increase strength.
Accepted editUseful but ambiguous: record the specific change, not blanket endorsement of the entire output.
Task success / passing testsEvidence of correctness, not proof of preferred wording, design or workflow.
No complaint / agent ratingNo inferred endorsement. Never allow the model to train on its own praise.
Conflicting recent evidenceShow counter-evidence. Prefer contextual splitting over erasing history without explanation.

Confidence is an explainable assessment, not a calibrated probability. Show source quality, independent observation count, diversity of contexts, recency and contradictions. A single explicit request may justify activation; many weak observations may not.

Resolution contract: respect the host’s instruction hierarchy and current explicit task instructions. Within Personal Brain, resolve authorized scope first, then applicability and specificity: accepted project exception, applicable specialist substyle, global baseline. Recency alone cannot override a pinned or explicitly confirmed rule. Surface unresolved conflicts instead of silently choosing.

Promotion rule: a project-specific pattern can suggest a global candidate, but global activation requires explicit approval. Do not infer all languages, frameworks or teams from one example.

06 · Consolidate, condense and forget

ActionMeaningRetention and recovery
ConsolidateCombine equivalent evidence and rules; retain one canonical identity with aliases or redirects.Preserve lineage while allowed; dependent links are remapped transactionally.
CondenseShorten active instructions while preserving behavior, conditions and exceptions.Keep versioned before/after and regression examples. Failed equivalence review leaves old version active.
SuppressStop applying an item or proposing a dismissed idea again.Reversible. Use minimal scoped suppression metadata; user can clear it.
ArchiveRemove an obsolete item from routine retrieval but keep it inspectable.Reversible. Archived dependencies must be resolved or flagged before activation of dependents.
Expire evidenceDelete temporary observations after their retention period.Approved abstract preferences may remain if policy allows; mark evidence as expired, never invent a source.
PurgeDelete selected content and its derived personal data.Irreversible after completion. Preview dependencies, summaries, indexes, exports and backups before confirmation.
Forgetting is a propagation operation. When evidence is purged, inspect all derived rules: remove dependent content or rebuild from independent retained evidence and quarantine anything unsupported. Purge associated cached packages, embeddings, indexes and snippets. An audit trail may retain a non-content deletion receipt only where policy permits.

Do not age out confirmed or pinned preferences merely because they have not been used recently. Decay applies to candidate relevance and stale evidence, not to the authority of a confirmed instruction. Backups must have a declared expiry or deletion mechanism. Restore must reapply deletion records before serving memory.

Copies already exported, pasted into prompts, held in active agent sessions or stored in third-party logs cannot be recalled by deleting the local record. Show those limits. A minimal suppression fingerprint reduces rediscovery but may itself be retained personal metadata; offer a separate “delete all suppression metadata” choice and explain that the idea may then be suggested again.

07 · User experience and operating modes

Learning inbox

Candidate sentence, affected style, exact diff, evidence and counter-evidence, scope, expected benefit, confidence reasons and dependency impact.

Actions: Accept, Edit, Project only, Split, Dismiss, Snooze. “Nothing to review” is a valid empty state.

Memory detail

Current instruction, applicability, pinned status, references, version history, applied-task history, source evidence and “why remembered.”

Actions: Edit, Restrict scope, Roll back, Archive, Forget. Expired evidence is labeled explicitly.

Learning health

Capture status by integration, pending conflicts, obsolete references, maintenance failures, budget consumption and recent forgetting receipts.

No vanity intelligence score. Surface observable events and actionable failures.

ModeBehavior
OffNo new capture or inference. Existing approved styles can still apply; disabling application is a separate control.
Suggest — proposed defaultCapture from enabled sources and build proposals. Inferred changes require review.
Explicit rememberA clear user request commits a scoped memory with a receipt; ambiguity asks for clarification.
Bounded automatic — later releaseOnly user-enabled change categories and scopes. Semantic changes stay reviewable; automatic metadata cleanup must preserve behavior. Includes pause, notification and rollback.

Every screen must support empty, loading, failure and successful states. Failed analysis preserves captured evidence and drafts; failed save preserves edits. Keyboard users can review evidence, compare diffs and approve without relying on color. Filter and back navigation retain context. Bulk approval reports partial failures rather than implying all changes succeeded.

08 · Data and processing contract

EntityMinimum fields / invariants
ObservationID; source event ID; actor/trust class; capture permission; project/session; timestamp; minimal evidence; task context; sensitivity; retention deadline. Unique event identity prevents duplicates.
CandidateID; target memory; proposed change; scope; evidence IDs; counter-evidence; confidence reasons; status; base version; extraction model/prompt version if applicable.
Memory revisionStable memory ID; revision; kind; content; applicability; exceptions; dependencies; reference IDs; provenance; approval actor/time; lifecycle; pin/expiry policy; content hash.
Application traceTask ID; host integration; requested scope; applied versions; excluded items/reasons; budget; conflicts; time. Content collection remains optional and minimized.
OutcomeApplication trace ID; user correction/acceptance; verification evidence; evaluator identity; ambiguity. Distinguish explicit feedback from inferred outcomes.
Lifecycle receiptAction; target IDs and versions; derivative handling; cache generation; completion/failure state; deletion scope. Never retain purged text in receipt fields.

Candidate lifecycle: observed → qualified → proposed → accepted / rejected / deferred / superseded. Accepted commits a memory revision. A stale proposal returns to review after rebasing; it is not applied over a newer user edit.

Memory lifecycle: active → revised / archived / suppressed → restored or purged. Purge is terminal for content. Rollback creates a new active revision rather than rewriting historical records.

Proposed operations: submit observation; list/review candidates; preview/apply a memory revision; resolve task context; report outcome; preview/execute forgetting; export/import. These are conceptual contracts, not existing API names. Each mutation requires authorization, idempotency and expected-version validation.

Start with the product’s existing persistence and job mechanisms after a code audit. A queue or vector database is not required by this design. Embeddings, if introduced, are derived indexes subject to the same access and deletion rules.

09 · Application across AI tools

  1. The adapter declares supported capture and application capabilities. Unsupported integrations show manual import/export rather than pretending to observe feedback.
  2. The agent supplies project identity, task activity and known languages/frameworks. The resolver validates scope and reads only authorized active memory.
  3. Resolve dependencies, specialist applicability and conflicts. Prefer compact relevant instructions; attach reference IDs for on-demand retrieval.
  4. Return a version-bound instruction package with “why included” explanations. Never silently truncate mandatory instructions: report budget overflow or request narrower context.
  5. The agent applies instructions to its task. Optional implementation/configuration changes use the normal repository change and verification workflow.
  6. Record outcomes against that package. A feedback event with the wrong version cannot be attributed as evidence for the current version.

When a package is stale or a record becomes unavailable, allow at most two rediscovery attempts for that task. Then continue with current explicit instructions and any still-valid scoped records, clearly reporting the gap. Never bypass a rejected selection by fetching and applying it through another path.

Learning runs at explicit capture, task completion, or a bounded maintenance schedule—not recursively on every self-generated summary. Each batch has event limits, cost/time budgets, retry limits, cancellation and a watermark. Failed model processing leaves observations retryable without duplicating accepted changes.

11 · Trust, privacy and failure behavior

ConcernRequired behavior
Instruction injectionTreat external documents, references and imported agent messages as data. They cannot authorize memory activation, permission changes or deletion.
Cross-project leakageEnforce scope server-side at every read/write, retrieval and export. Test similarly named projects and absent/incorrect scope IDs.
Secrets and sensitive dataMinimize before storage. Reject credentials from memory content; provide user review for sensitive evidence and per-source exclusions.
External model processingShow destination and data categories before enabling remote extraction. Local persistence does not imply local inference. Keep model credentials outside memory.
Concurrent updatesExpected-version checks; atomic commit; conflict diff for user resolution. No last-write-wins loss of user edits.
Offline or unavailable providerApproved local memory remains usable. Queue permitted observations within budget; expose backlog and retry status.
Corrupt import / missing dependencyValidate schema and size, preview conflicts, reject or quarantine incomplete bundles. Never replace active memory partially.
Bad consolidationKeep previous revision active until approved; preserve evidence links and test examples. Offer rollback and targeted candidate rejection.
Deletion during applicationNew retrieval excludes deleted items; invalidate packages and notify connected consumers where supported. Already delivered context cannot be erased remotely.
Runaway learningNo self-generated evidence loops; bounded batch processing and independent-event counting. User pause stops new inference and activation jobs.

12 · Release acceptance and measurement

GateRequired test / expected result
A01 · Remember and persistenceExplicit scoped request survives restart; another project cannot retrieve project-only content.
A02 · Evidence integrityReplay one event from two adapters; one contribution remains. Quoted “always do X” cannot activate a preference.
A03 · DiagnosisCorrection against a loaded rule is classified as an execution issue unless the meaning changes.
A04 · Condensation equivalenceBefore/after fixtures retain obligations, exceptions, negatives and applicability; failed fixtures prevent activation.
A05 · Conflict / concurrencyTwo edits from the same base cannot overwrite each other; conflict is surfaced with drafts preserved.
A06 · RetrievalLanguage/project fixtures return expected versions and exclusions; mandatory budget overflow is visible.
A07 · Forget propagationPurge fixture is absent from primary records, derivatives, caches and rebuilt indexes. Restore honors deletion receipts.
A08 · PortabilityExport/import round trip preserves scope, references and version provenance; unavailable references remain flagged.
A09 · RecoveryProvider outage and interrupted job resume without duplicate activation; retry cap prevents infinite rediscovery.
A10 · User interfaceKeyboard and desktop/phone walkthroughs cover review, conflict, rollback and forgetting; destructive consequences remain visible.
A11 · Application parityEach supported adapter receives equivalent applicable memory for the same context; unsupported features are declared.
A12 · Evaluation integrityOutcome records bind to actual applied versions; no-feedback tasks do not count as positive preference evidence.

Measure: accepted proposals / reviewed proposals; repeat corrections per comparable task where the rule was actually applied; reversions / activated revisions; irrelevant inclusions / reviewed applications; unresolved conflict age; active context size; retrieval latency; forgetting completion latency; and capture-to-review delay. Report denominators and observation windows.

Proposed release gate: all deterministic acceptance fixtures pass, no known cross-scope disclosure or failed deletion propagation, and a user pilot confirms that suggestions are useful. Product owner and engineering lead set latency, retention and cost targets from measured workloads before rollout. No numerical improvement is claimed in this proposal.

13 · Implementation roadmap and decisions

PhaseDeliverableExit condition
0 · Audit and contractMap existing records, feedback, versions, project boundaries, adapters and export behavior. Confirm data ownership and migration paths.Reviewed gap map and schema/API plan; no duplicate storage subsystem introduced without need.

Proposed decision D-03: Start with reviewed learning and explicit remember commands. Defer model fine-tuning, a hosted sync service, an autonomous code-changing agent, and general-purpose transcript storage. They are not needed to prove the loop.

Proposed decision D-04: Forgetting and scope isolation ship in phase 1, not as later cleanup features. Their consequences: more lifecycle work upfront, but no unbounded memory accumulation.

Open decisions and owners: product owner chooses default retention, supported knowledge classes and automatic categories; privacy/security owner approves remote processing and backup deletion policy; engineering lead confirms existing storage reuse and adapter support; user pilot determines notification cadence. Until decided: no inferred auto-activation, no remote extraction by default, and no claim of complete backup purge.
